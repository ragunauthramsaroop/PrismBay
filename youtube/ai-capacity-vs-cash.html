Cash savings = verified reductions in overtime, contractors and other spending minus incremental AI costs. A negative result means spending increases. Released capacity is an opportunity measure, not an additional cash saving.

How to use the result

In the starting hypothetical example, 100 released hours at $36 per hour represent $3,600 of potential capacity value. Payroll and outside spending remain unchanged, while AI costs $400 per month. The net cash outcome is $400 of additional monthly spending, not $3,600 saved.

In the alternative hypothetical example, verified overtime spending falls $1,000 per month and AI costs $400. Net monthly cash spending falls $600. Do not add the $3,600 capacity figure to the $600 cash figure. Check whether the same hours drove both estimates.

Before approving a project, verify a documented pre-AI baseline, accepted output quality, the source of every spending reduction, implementation costs, and whether released hours are redeployed to measurable work. Capacity and cash outcomes serve different decisions.

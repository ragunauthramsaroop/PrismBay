# Worker 298 — AI capex, earnings quality, risk and scenarios

Status: manuscript input. Command #291. Worker #298.

## Central thesis

The AI profit chain divides into two economic positions. NVIDIA and TSMC monetize infrastructure demand directly. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta fund large infrastructure programs to support cloud, ads, productivity and model deployment. Apple remains materially less capital intensive and should not be judged by the same data-center build metric.

The key forensic question is whether incremental cash returns from AI-related services and advertising exceed the step-up in infrastructure spend after depreciation, stock-based compensation, lease costs and working capital are included.

## Earnings-quality diagnostics

For each hyperscaler publish:

1. Operating cash flow / net income.
2. FCF / net income using a clearly defined capex and lease treatment.
3. Cash capex / revenue.
4. Stock-based compensation / revenue and / operating cash flow.
5. Depreciation / cash capex.
6. Net share count change after buybacks and stock compensation.
7. Segment operating income where disclosed.

Do not compare company-defined FCF across firms without reconciling lease and asset-sale treatments.

## Depreciation-lag test

A large capex increase affects cash immediately and accounting earnings over the useful life of the assets. Therefore the paper should show two curves: cash infrastructure spend and depreciation expense. If capex rises much faster than depreciation, current operating income carries a timing benefit relative to future periods, all else equal.

This is an accounting timing observation, not a claim that earnings are misstated.

## Monetization evidence hierarchy

Strongest evidence: disclosed segment revenue and operating income directly tied to the infrastructure cycle.

Moderate evidence: management disclosure that AI contributes to growth, paired with revenue or backlog metrics but no incremental margin.

Weak evidence: product launches, model usage or user counts without revenue and margin linkage.

No paper section should equate usage growth with profit proof.

## Scenario architecture, 2027-2030

These are assumption sets, not forecasts.

Downside:
- hyperscaler capex remains high for longer than monetization growth
- cloud and AI pricing compresses
- server or accelerator utilization underperforms expectations
- NVIDIA Data Center growth normalizes sharply
- TSMC advanced-node utilization softens
- export restrictions tighten
- higher depreciation catches up with the 2025-2026 capex wave

Base:
- revenue growth moderates but infrastructure utilization rises
- capex intensity stabilizes after the current build phase
- cloud and advertising margins absorb higher depreciation
- semiconductor demand remains strong but less hyper-growth driven

Upside:
- AI services create sustained incremental cloud, productivity and advertising revenue
- utilization remains high
- custom silicon and accelerators expand the total market rather than purely displacing one another
- operating leverage offsets rising depreciation and power costs

## Sensitivity table design

For each hyperscaler test capex intensity at base, base +3 percentage points and base -3 points.

Test a one-year reduction in server useful life as an earnings sensitivity. This changes depreciation timing, not cash flow.

Test cloud or AI pricing at base, -5% and +5% while holding volume assumptions explicit.

For NVIDIA and TSMC, test AI demand growth, utilization and gross-margin compression rather than hyperscaler-style capex intensity.

## Profit durability scorecard

Use four grades only: Strong, Adequate, Weak, Insufficient disclosure. Do not produce a false-precision decimal score.

Criteria:
- cash backing
- capital intensity trend
- monetization auditability
- balance-sheet resilience
- customer/supplier/geographic concentration
- regulatory and export-control exposure
- segment diversification
- dependence on non-operating income or one-off items

## Board and investor implications

Boards should review cash ROIC alongside accounting operating income during heavy infrastructure build periods.

Capital-allocation analysis should compare buybacks and dividends against FCF after full cash capex, not against operating income alone.

AI monetization claims should be tied to measurable segment economics wherever disclosure permits.

The final white paper should keep scenarios adjacent to their assumptions so readers never confuse scenario arithmetic with company guidance.

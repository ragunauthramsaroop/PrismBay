## Executive judgment

The original infographic is broadly accurate as a latest-reported-profit snapshot, but it is not one synchronized TTM ranking. Its figures draw from at least four fiscal windows and several distinct accounting/economic models. A defensible publication must therefore separate three questions: absolute reported profit, quality and repeatability of that profit, and durability of the underlying profit engine.

The most important correction to the earlier synthesis is Saudi Aramco. The infographic's $92.8B reconciles to 2025 net income attributable to Aramco shareholders of $92.811B after rounding. Total group net income was $93.389B. The difference is ownership basis, not an infographic error.

A second major finding is that synchronization alone does not solve comparability. The independently calculated LTM panel to mid-2026 pushes Alphabet and NVIDIA sharply higher, but Alphabet's LTM result is heavily affected by gains on debt and equity securities. Timing comparability improves while earnings-quality comparability becomes more important.

## Board-level findings

1. Scale of profit and quality of profit are separate dimensions. Alphabet, Berkshire and Microsoft all show material non-operating or investment-related effects that require bridges from operating income to net income.
2. AI infrastructure creates a cash-before-depreciation timing gap. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta are spending cash at a pace that will flow into depreciation over future periods. Current operating profit therefore needs to be read with forward depreciation and utilization risk.
3. NVIDIA and TSMC are the clearest direct monetizers of the AI build cycle, but they carry different risk. NVIDIA is fabless and exposed to demand concentration, pricing and export controls. TSMC carries the foundry capital burden, utilization risk and Taiwan concentration.
4. Apple's profit engine remains unusually asset-light relative to hyperscalers. Services mix and share repurchases support earnings per share without a comparable data-center build.
5. Aramco's profit durability depends on price, production, fiscal take and capital discipline. 2025 distributions were close to company-defined FCF, while gearing remained low.
6. Amazon and Meta show the clearest 2025 examples of higher operating cash generation being absorbed by infrastructure spending.
7. JPMorgan requires a bank-specific lens. CET1, ROTCE, credit costs and funding strength matter more than industrial operating margin or conventional FCF.
8. Berkshire's GAAP net income should never be treated as a clean operating-profit proxy because mark-to-market investment gains and impairments can move the headline by tens of billions.

## Red flags that must remain visible

- Mixed fiscal windows in the original ranking.
- Alphabet securities gains materially affect 2025 and especially H1 2026 net income.
- Berkshire GAAP investment gains and impairments distort operating comparability.
- Microsoft GAAP and non-GAAP OpenAI investment effects must stay separated.
- Amazon and Meta FCF definitions require lease and asset-purchase treatment notes.
- TSMC USD conversion must cite the company's disclosed USD figure or state the independently calculated FX method.
- Aramco group net income and shareholder-attributable net income must never be conflated.
- JPMorgan must not be placed in an industrial operating-margin leaderboard.
- Scenario outputs are assumptions, not forecasts.

## Publication design brief

Use an institutional white-paper format, not a promotional brochure. Deep navy, white and restrained gold accents. Each major part begins with a divider page. Tables use compact typography, alternating fills and explicit unit/period headers. Charts should avoid decorative effects and show the exact source below the figure. Use callout boxes only for audit verdicts, accounting traps, and board implications.

Every chart or table must show: source period, currency/unit, assurance status, and whether the metric is reported or independently calculated.

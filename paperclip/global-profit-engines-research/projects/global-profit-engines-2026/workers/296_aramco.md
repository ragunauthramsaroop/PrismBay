## Ownership-basis reconciliation

The infographic's $92.8B is not an error if it represents profit attributable to Aramco shareholders. The audited 2025 financial statements report total group net income of $93.389B and net income attributable to shareholders of $92.811B. Rounded to one decimal place, the attributable figure is $92.8B.

This distinction must remain explicit in every table. Group net income, shareholder-attributable net income and adjusted net income are different measures.

## Profit-quality interpretation

Aramco's reported profit is structurally different from platform, semiconductor and hardware companies. Oil and gas prices, production policy, sovereign royalties and taxes, upstream volume, downstream margins and capital discipline all shape the result.

The 2025 realized crude price fell materially while hydrocarbon production rose. Net income therefore reflects both weaker price realization and higher production volumes. The report should avoid attributing year-on-year profit movement to one factor without a formal price-volume-fiscal bridge.

Adjusted net income and free cash flow are non-IFRS measures and must never replace audited IFRS profit in the headline ranking. They belong in a separate operating-quality section.

## Distribution coverage

2025 base and performance-linked dividends paid totaled about $85.453B, close to reported non-IFRS FCF of $85.428B. The near one-for-one relationship makes distribution coverage a first-order capital-allocation issue. The analysis should discuss this as a cash-allocation observation, not as a claim of stress, because gearing remained low at 3.8%.

## Sensitivity architecture

Downside case assumptions: realized crude price lower by $15-$20/bbl from the base, production down 3%-5%, capex 10%-15% higher, and no offset from downstream margins. Test effects on net income, operating cash flow, FCF and gearing.

Base case: use disclosed production and capex guidance with stable fiscal terms. Present as an assumption set, not a forecast.

Upside case: realized price higher by $15-$20/bbl, production normalization or growth, and disciplined capex. Test the same four outputs.

Do not estimate tax or royalty elasticities without sourcing the fiscal formula from the annual report.

## Strategic and transition risks

OPEC+ and Saudi production policy affect volumes independently of market demand.

The fiscal regime creates a sovereign take through taxes and royalties before dividends to the state as shareholder. These legs should remain separate.

The capital program, including gas expansion, upstream projects, refining and chemicals, affects future depreciation and FCF.

Energy-transition analysis should distinguish operational emissions, demand-transition risk and downstream/chemicals strategy. Avoid unsupported claims about the pace of global oil displacement.

## Original worked example and arithmetic

All values are hypothetical, not customer outcomes. A company releases 100 staff hours at an assumed $36 fully loaded hourly rate, equivalent to $3,600 of *capacity value*. If payroll and contractor spend do not change, this is **not** a $3,600 cash saving. If the new software costs $400/month and all other expenditures remain unchanged, monthly cash spending increases by $400. In a **separate alternative scenario**, a verified $1,000 monthly overtime reduction minus the $400 software expense yields a $600 monthly net cash spending reduction. The two scenarios are alternatives and must not be added together. Avoid double-counting the same staff hours as both cash savings and redeployed capacity.

Relevant evidence:
- CFO.com, October 20, 2026: https://www.cfo.com/news/a-saved-hour-from-ai-is-not-yet-a-cash-saving/833361/
- Deloitte Global, October 22, 2025: https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/what-we-do/capabilities/ai/research/ai-roi-the-paradox-of-rising-investment-and-elusive-returns.html
- These sources support the accounting distinction and need for ROI measurement, not the illustrative dollar amounts.

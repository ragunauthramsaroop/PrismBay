## Experiment S2: Your AI task costs 40 cents, or does it?
- Audience: operators, finance leaders and automation buyers.
- Hypothesis: the acceptance-rate denominator creates an immediately useful financial insight and qualified subscriptions.
- Target length: 40–50 seconds. Hook type: numerical surprise. First frame: **$0.40 ≠ $1.25**. Disclosure: FICTIONAL EXAMPLE.
- Title A: `The AI Cost Metric That Hides Failed Work`.
- Title B for a later sequential test: `Why Your 40-Cent AI Task Costs $1.25`.
- Cover frame: $0.40 crossed out, $1.25 large, label PER ACCEPTED RESULT.
- Full original narration (approx. 109 words):

"Your AI dashboard says forty cents per task. Your accepted result costs one dollar and twenty-five cents. Watch the denominator. This fictional workflow attempts one hundred tasks. Only eighty pass the quality check. Model and tool charges total forty dollars, so the dashboard shows forty cents per attempt. But a reviewer spends another sixty dollars checking and fixing the work. Total operating cost is one hundred dollars. Divide by the eighty accepted results. The true measured cost in this example is one dollar and twenty-five cents per accepted result. Count failed tasks and review time before claiming AI savings. Follow PrismBay AI for practical AI return-on-investment math."

- Original scene sequence: 0–2s two competing prices; 2–9s 100 attempts counter; 9–15s 80 accepted, 20 failed; 15–23s $40 tool bill; 23–31s +$60 human review; 31–41s $100 ÷ 80 = $1.25; 41–end takeaway and CTA.
- Source of all numbers: explicitly fictional arithmetic; no customer performance claim. $40/100 attempts = $0.40 per attempt; ($40+$60)/80 accepted = $1.25 per accepted output.
- CTA: follow for defensible AI ROI measurement.

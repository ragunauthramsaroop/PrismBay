## Full narration and visual script

### 0:00–0:50 | The trap

Your AI vendor says you own your data. Good. Now ask a harder question: If you terminate the agreement on Friday, can your team operate on Monday? Ownership of records is not the same as operational independence. A chatbot may export a conversation while leaving the prompts, automations, approval rules, integrations, and audit trail trapped behind a subscription. Today we will run a five-part exit test. You can use this before signing a contract or during a renewal. We will not rank vendors by marketing promises. We will ask for evidence, time the exercise, and document the gaps.

[Visual: Friday CANCEL -> Monday OPERATE; the gap fills with five proof cards.]

### 0:50–2:05 | Proof one: export usable records

Start with a fictional procurement assistant. It receives supplier inquiries, extracts fields, drafts replies, and routes exceptions to a human. Request a complete export of a representative set of ten test cases. You need the original inputs, generated outputs, timestamps, source references, user actions, and final dispositions. A screenshot of a dashboard is not an export. Ask for a machine-readable format, a field dictionary, and a sample import into an independent system. Validate ten records against the originals, including two exceptions. Count missing fields. If an export is technically available but takes thirty days, record the operational delay.

[Visual: CSV + JSON + schema -> independent viewer; counter 10/10.]

### 2:05–3:10 | Proof two: recover the workflow

The records are only half the story. Ask for the operating instructions: prompt versions, routing rules, approval checkpoints, exception categories, data mappings, and integration diagrams. A new team should be able to understand what happens when the AI is uncertain, when a supplier record conflicts with the database, or when a human rejects a recommendation. The objective is not to demand a vendor's proprietary model weights or trade secrets. It is to recover the parts of the workflow your organization owns and the specifications needed to rebuild the service. Separate vendor intellectual property from customer-specific configuration in the contract.

[Visual: INPUT -> CLASSIFY -> HUMAN APPROVAL -> LOG -> OUTPUT; highlight ownership boundary.]

### 3:10–4:25 | Proof three: verify access and deletion

Next, draw a data map. Where does the vendor store prompts, uploaded files, embeddings, logs, backups, and metadata? Which subprocessors receive access? How long does each category remain after termination? Ask for written retention and deletion commitments, including backups, exceptions, and evidence of completion. If the system has access to supplier bank details or internal financial files, your security team should also verify permissions, credentials, and the revocation procedure. Deleting an account is not equivalent to proving every copy is gone. The contractual response must identify limits rather than promising impossible instant deletion.

[Visual: customer -> vendor -> subprocessor -> backup; expiry clocks and revoke button.]

### 4:25–5:35 | Proof four: keep the audit trail

Imagine an AI agent recommends paying an invoice and a human approves it. Six months later, finance needs to reconstruct the decision. The export should preserve who reviewed the recommendation, what supporting information was visible, the version of the workflow, and what action occurred. Test whether logs remain readable after the vendor portal is unavailable. You do not need every internal model computation. You need enough evidence to explain and investigate the business action. Define the audit retention period according to your organization's legal and operational obligations. Do not assume a model transcript alone satisfies those obligations.

[Visual: invoice -> recommendation -> named approver -> decision -> timestamped log.]

### 5:35–6:45 | Proof five: rehearse continuity

Now run the actual exit. In a safe test environment, disable the vendor connection for one representative process. Restore the last known configuration in a backup system or documented manual procedure. Measure recovery time, lost work, error rate, and the people required. Define a maximum acceptable outage before the test. For a customer support draft assistant, a day of manual processing might be tolerable. For a payment control, a missing approval checkpoint may be unacceptable even for one transaction. The correct continuity plan depends on business impact, not the number of features in a demo.

[Visual: timer starts; alternate path activates; checklist "no unauthorized action".]

### 6:45–7:55 | The scorecard and a realistic example

Give each proof a simple result: PASS, CONDITIONAL, or FAIL. A pass requires a demonstrated artifact, not a salesperson's assurance. In our fictional example, records export correctly, workflow documentation is incomplete, deletion terms are unclear, audit logs are portable, and continuity takes two days against a one-day target. The result is two passes, one conditional, and two failures. That is not a universal risk score. It is a decision record. The procurement owner assigns remediation actions, deadlines, and contractual conditions. Critical failures involving sensitive data or authorization should stop deployment until addressed.

[Visual: 5-row matrix; green, amber, red; label EXAMPLE ONLY.]

### 7:55–9:00 | The commercial decision

A low subscription price can conceal a costly exit. Before committing, ask who pays for export support, whether there is an API or bulk download limit, how long access continues after notice, and which service levels apply during transition. Request the exit procedure in writing and run a limited rehearsal before a multi-year contract. Tie final acceptance to evidence. This approach does not prohibit proprietary AI. It makes the dependency visible and negotiable. NIST's voluntary AI Risk Management Framework includes third-party risk, contingency planning, and safe decommissioning among its governance considerations. Our five-proof test is a practical translation of those principles, not a claim of NIST certification.

[Visual: contract clause callouts: EXPORT, ASSISTANCE, TRANSITION, DELETION, LOGS.]

### 9:00–9:25 | Close

The right question is not "Does the AI demo work?" It is "Can the business recover when the relationship ends?" Before your next renewal, ask for one real export and one timed continuity test. If the answer is unclear, you have found a procurement issue while you still have leverage. PrismBay AI helps founders, operators, and executives implement measurable AI business systems. Subscribe for the next practical control test.

[Visual: OWN THE PROCESS. TEST THE EXIT.]

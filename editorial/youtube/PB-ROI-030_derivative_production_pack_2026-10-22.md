Title: **AI Saved 67 Hours. Why Did the CFO Record $0?**
Hypothesis: an apparent contradiction will attract more qualified viewers and saves than a generic productivity claim.
CTA: "Follow PrismBay AI for measurable AI business systems."
Voice script (~116 words, adjust to natural pacing):
"Your AI pilot saved sixty-seven staff hours. Your CFO recorded zero dollars in savings. Both figures can be correct. Here's the distinction. Suppose one thousand supplier requests took twelve minutes each before AI, and eight minutes after. You released about sixty-seven hours of staff capacity. That's valuable. But if payroll, overtime, and external invoices stay the same, you have not yet reduced a cash expense. Put the result in two separate ledgers: capacity released, and verified cash avoided. If the team clears a backlog, measure that operational result too. Don't sell potential salary value as cash savings. Show the actual invoice or payroll change. That's how you build an AI business case finance will trust."

## SHORT C | PB-ROI-030-S3 | The three-gate stop rule
Experiment: PILOT-GATE-C
Audience: procurement directors, risk owners, executives sponsoring AI pilots.
Hook: "A faster process is not enough to scale."
Format: 9:16, 45–55 seconds; three-stage traffic-light decision flow, not the ledger or waterfall visuals.
Title: **Three Tests Before You Scale an AI Agent**
Hypothesis: concrete decision rules attract subscribers who want governance systems rather than news.
Voice script (~105 words):
"Your AI pilot is faster. Should you scale it? Not until it passes three gates. Gate one: value. Are the savings verified, or is the result only capacity released? Both matter, but keep them separate. Gate two: quality. Did error rates, rework, and customer outcomes stay within the limits you agreed before testing? Gate three: control. Can you prove who approved each sensitive action, what evidence the agent used, and how to stop the workflow? If one gate fails, modify the pilot or stop. If all three pass, consider a controlled expansion. AI success is an operating decision, not a demo."
Scene plan:
00:00-00:02: Large "FASTER ≠ READY" with three empty gate slots.
00:02-00:17: Value gate; real receipt versus capacity meter.
00:17-00:31: Quality gate; sample queue and error-rate gauge.
00:31-00:46: Control gate; approval chain and stop switch, no vendor UI copied.
00:46-00:55: Decision fork SCALE / MODIFY / STOP, brand promise.
Cover: "3 GATES" and original decision fork.
Success: subscriber conversion and thoughtful comments; avoid relying on view count alone.
